## The radiology template

Diagnostics are already superhuman on narrow imaging tasks — and radiologist employment *grew*.

Two reasons, and both generalize: reading images was never the whole job, and **liability doesn't transfer to a model.** That is the template for every professional domain in this document.

AI compresses the diagnostic and documentation layers, leaving licensure, liability, physical examination, and procedure as the human moat.

## The binding constraint is not intelligence

It is **supply of licensed capacity and reimbursement rules.** Healthcare has never been bottlenecked on knowing what to do; it is bottlenecked on who is permitted to do it and who pays. Adding intelligence to a system constrained on authorization changes less than it should.

## Where the change actually lands

Expect enormous **consumer-side** change: AI as first-line triage, roughly free, and better than the median GP visit for informational purposes. This routes *around* the formal system faster than it changes it — the same pattern as every prior information technology in medicine, but at much larger magnitude.

The gap between what a motivated patient can know and what the system will act on becomes a significant source of friction and political pressure.

## Drug discovery

Real acceleration in target identification and structure prediction. But **~60% of clinical failures are efficacy/toxicity in humans**, which AI cannot predict without better biology.

> Expect **~20–30% cost reduction in preclinical**, and minimal change to the 10-year, $2B clinical gauntlet before ~2032.

The rate limiter is wet-lab throughput and trial recruitment — which is why **automated labs matter more than better models.** Same conclusion as [Science](science.md), for the same reason.
