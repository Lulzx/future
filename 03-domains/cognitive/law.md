Document review, discovery, contract analysis, and first-draft everything are gone as billable human hours — or will be, on the commercial schedule of tool adoption rather than model release.

## Why this domain is structurally exposed

The profession's economics rest on the **billable hour** — which is a direct measure of the input AI destroys. Most professions sell an outcome and are disrupted indirectly. Law sells time, and time is exactly what is being compressed. There is no layer of indirection to absorb the shock.

Worse, the leverage model compounds it: BigLaw profitability requires a pyramid of juniors billing at a multiple of their cost. Remove the juniors' work and the pyramid does not simply shrink — the economics of the whole firm structure change. That is the [apprenticeship gap](../../02-games/4-labor.md) in pure form, visible earliest here and in software.

## What to expect

- **Brutal restructuring of leverage models.** The pyramid needs juniors to be profitable; the work that made juniors profitable is the most automatable work in the building.
- **Large expansion of access to legal services at the bottom.** Huge welfare gain, poorly measured — the counterfactual is people who previously received no legal help and appear in no statistic. → [Global South](../contested/geopolitics/global-south.md) pattern (welfare ≠ growth) applied domestically.
- **Continued human control** of anything requiring standing before a court, liability exposure, or negotiation with a human counterparty.

## The pattern

Same as [medicine](../physical/medicine/): the analytical layer compresses, the accountability layer does not. What survives is not what is hardest to do — it is what someone must be answerable for.

## Layers by ground-truth cost

| Layer | Ground truth | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Document review / e-discovery | Cheap — relevance labels, production sets | Now; largely done at the frontier |
| Contract draft / playbook work | Moderate — clause libraries, playbook match | Now |
| Research memos | Moderate — cites checkable; judgment less so | Now–2028 |
| Negotiation | Expensive — counterparty is human, path-dependent | Slow |
| Courtroom advocacy / standing | Institutional monopoly | Human until rules change |
| Liability for advice | [Insurance](insurance.md) + professional rules | Sets deployment frontier |

**Ground truth is cheap on text comparison and expensive on strategy.** That is why the middle hollows first and the partnership layer sells accountability long after associates' hours vanish.

## The moat is financial, then institutional

Reread with [insurance](insurance.md): the lawyer's moat is not reading speed — it is that a malpractice policy and a bar card exist for a human and do not yet cover an autonomous system. Underwriting reprices faster than the bar rewrites ethics opinions. → [Uncertainty 6](../../06-uncertainties/correlated-risk.md), [C6](../../07-indicators/governance.md)

Courts and bars can also **mandate** human sign-off (they will, after incidents). That entrenches the accountability layer even when capability would allow more. The reverse — broad unauthorized-practice expansion for AI tools — is the access-to-justice path and is politically contested.

## Apprenticeship and education

Law is a canary for [Game 4](../../02-games/4-labor.md):

- Junior associate tasks = ideal AI tasks
- Partnership track = scarce residual
- Law schools still sell three years of credential while the first two years of firm work disappear

[Education](education.md) + [meaning](meaning.md): credential collapse meets missing junior rungs → status shock for a profession built on tournament ranking. Uncertainty 3's inversion (AI as dense mentor) is testable here first: supervised AI-augmented junior years vs pure headcount cuts.

> **Watch:** entry-level : senior posting ratios in large firms; first-year class sizes vs BigLaw offer rates. Same indicator family as [B1](../../07-indicators/diffusion.md).

## Access expansion vs quality risk

Bottom-of-pyramid legal help (consumer contracts, benefits, small claims) sees **volume explode** with tool-assisted self-help and low-cost clinics. Failures concentrate where:

- The user cannot evaluate bad advice ([Game 5](../../02-games/5-information.md) lemons in legal text)
- Liability is unclear (tool vendor vs user vs "not legal advice" disclaimers)
- The case needed negotiation or court craft, not a form

Regulation will oscillate between **access** and **unauthorized practice**. Expect jurisdiction variance large enough that "AI law" is a state-level story in the US before it is a federal one — parallel to [energy](../../01-substrate/energy.md) ratepayer politics.

## Failure modes

- **If outcome-priced legal products** with real indemnification scale, the billable-hour exposure claim weakens and the profession restructures around products faster.
- **If bars ban effective tools**, access gains stall and elite firms capture internal tools only — inequality up.
- **If liability shifts to model providers**, the accountability moat thins the way medicine's would. → [C6](../../07-indicators/governance.md)
