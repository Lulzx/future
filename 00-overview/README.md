## The spine

Everything in this corpus descends from two sentences colliding:

> **Capability grows fastest where verification is cheap.**
> **Value accrues to whatever intelligence cannot manufacture.**

The first is a claim about the technology, forced by [data exhaustion](../01-substrate/data.md) pushing the frontier onto verifiable-reward training. The second is a claim about economics, forced by [inference cost collapse](../01-substrate/inference-economics.md) making cognition itself abundant.

The first explains the *uneven* progress across [every domain](../03-domains/) - why math and code raced ahead of taste and judgment. The second explains *where the money goes* - to energy, land, licenses, distribution, trust, and physical presence rather than to the technology or to most of the firms adopting it.

Together they produce the document's least intuitive conclusion: **the transition is real, large, and mostly invisible in the aggregate statistics**, because competitive markets pass the gains to consumers and the concentrated harms land on cohorts and regions rather than on national averages.

Note what the spine does *not* claim. It does not say capability stops growing where verification is expensive - it says growth there is slower and gated on buying cheaper ground truth ([data](../01-substrate/data.md) names the escape routes). And it does not say the scarce-complement list is fixed - [Uncertainty 7](../06-uncertainties/complement-erosion.md) exists precisely because that list has a half-life. The spine is a claim about *gradients*, not endpoints, which is why it can be checked quarterly ([Part VII](../07-indicators/)) rather than only at the finish line.

Each sentence has a designated adversary. [Uncertainty 5](../06-uncertainties/learned-verification.md) attacks the first: if learned verifiers get good enough, the cheap-verification gradient flattens and the domain ordering in Part III reshuffles. [Uncertainty 7](../06-uncertainties/complement-erosion.md) attacks the second: intelligence plus capital can, over time, manufacture substitutes for several items on the complement list, and every distributional prediction in [Part IX](../09-macro/) is exposed to how fast that happens. A reader who wants to break the corpus efficiently should start with those two pages; everything else is downstream.

## Why two compression ratios

Compression is not a convenience here; it is a test. An argument that survives being cut to one page ([thesis](thesis.md)) and then to one paragraph ([compressed](compressed.md)) has a spine; one that only works at full length is a mood. The two short forms also serve as commitment devices - they state the conclusion baldly enough that a miss cannot be reinterpreted afterward, which is what [scoring](../08-method/scoring.md) needs. If the long form and the short form ever disagree, the short form is the claim and the long form has drifted; that check belongs to the Review step of every [autoresearch round](../RESEARCH.md).

## Reading orders

- **Linear** - follow the **Next** link at the foot of each page, starting with [Thesis](thesis.md). Runs about 90 minutes.
- **Argument-first** - [Thesis](thesis.md) → [Part I](../01-substrate/) → [Part II](../02-games/) → stop. That is the half that carries the argument.
- **Sceptic-first** - [Part VI](../06-uncertainties/) and [Part VIII](../08-method/) before anything else, to see where it is weakest and how the numbers were made.
- **Operator-first** - [Compressed](compressed.md) → [Game 3](../02-games/3-firms.md) → [Part VII](../07-indicators/), for what to build and what to watch.

Each order has a failure mode worth knowing before you pick. Linear front-loads substrate detail and can bury the economic punchline for a reader who only wanted the conclusion. Argument-first stops before [Part VI](../06-uncertainties/), so it delivers the case without the counter-case - do not cite the corpus from that read alone. Sceptic-first inverts that risk: uncertainties read as fatal when you have not yet seen the structure they are attacking. Operator-first optimizes for action and quietly assumes the framework is right; an operator who skips the [dependency index](dependencies.md) will not notice when a miss upstream invalidates the thing they are building. There is no order without a blind spot - the orders exist so you can choose which one you accept.
