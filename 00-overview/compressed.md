# The Compressed Version

← [Index](../README.md) · [Thesis](thesis.md)

---

Intelligence becomes cheap and abundant; everything that intelligence cannot manufacture becomes correspondingly precious. Energy, land, trust, verification, licensure, physical presence, and genuine human relationships appreciate. Competent cognitive labor depreciates - especially the junior rung that used to make experts. Firms must adopt and mostly won't profit from it; consumers capture most of the surplus in forms the statistics won't measure well. Aggregate job loss is easier to absorb than it looks ([demography](../09-macro/demography.md)); the apprenticeship gap is not. And the deepest structural risk is that our decision-making institutions - legal, political, diplomatic - run at human deliberation speed while the systems they govern increasingly do not.

**The single most useful thing to build in this environment is anything that makes verification cheap, since that is the scarce good the entire equilibrium now rotates around.**

Concretely, that means:

- **Cryptographic provenance at the point of capture** - attestation in the sensor, not inference after the fact → [C8](../07-indicators/governance.md)
- **Institutional vouching with skin in the game** - reputation that costs something to stake
- **Synchronous high-stakes evaluation** to replace the dead asynchronous signals
- **Systems that preserve legible human accountability** at exactly the points where liability and judgment still bind
- **Balance sheets and underwriting** that can price residual risk - or deployment stops where insurance refuses → [Uncertainty 6](../06-uncertainties/correlated-risk.md)

These are the complements cheap cognition cannot supply itself, and they are undersupplied for the same reason safety is - each is a public good with private cost, so the market will build them late, and the first one to build them well will own a great deal.

If the paragraph above is wrong, it is most likely wrong in one of two ways: verification itself gets automated, so it never becomes the scarce good ([Uncertainty 5](../06-uncertainties/learned-verification.md)); or the list of things intelligence cannot manufacture shrinks faster than the decade this advice assumes ([Uncertainty 7](../06-uncertainties/complement-erosion.md)). Watch those two pages; the rest of the corpus is elaboration.

A third way to be wrong is quieter: **Taiwan soft-fails** without a war ([Uncertainty 4](../06-uncertainties/taiwan.md) gray zone) and every timeline slips in quarters while the spine still looks intact. Watch freight insurance and license denials, not only fleets.
