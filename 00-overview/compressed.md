# The Compressed Version

← [Index](../README.md) · [Thesis](thesis.md)

---

Intelligence becomes cheap and abundant; everything that intelligence cannot manufacture becomes correspondingly precious. Energy, land, trust, verification, licensure, physical presence, and genuine human relationships appreciate. Competent cognitive labor depreciates. Firms must adopt and mostly won't profit from it; consumers capture most of the surplus in forms the statistics won't measure well. The transition's worst harm is not unemployment but the quiet destruction of the pathway by which novices become experts. And the deepest structural risk is that our decision-making institutions — legal, political, diplomatic — run at human deliberation speed while the systems they govern increasingly do not.

**The single most useful thing to build in this environment is anything that makes verification cheap, since that is the scarce good the entire equilibrium now rotates around.**

Concretely, that means:

- **Cryptographic provenance at the point of capture** — attestation in the sensor, not inference after the fact
- **Institutional vouching with skin in the game** — reputation that costs something to stake
- **Synchronous high-stakes evaluation** to replace the dead asynchronous signals
- **Systems that preserve legible human accountability** at exactly the points where liability and judgment still bind

These are the load-bearing complements to cheap cognition, and they are undersupplied for the same reason safety is — each is a public good with private cost, so the market will build them late, and the first one to build them well will own a great deal.
