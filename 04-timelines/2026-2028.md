# 2026–2028 — the agentic transition and the capex test

Models become reliable enough for **multi-hour autonomous work in verifiable domains** — the qualifier doing real work there, per [the data asymmetry](../01-substrate/data.md).

## What changes

- **The purchasing unit shifts from seats to outcomes**, breaking a lot of software business models. Per-seat pricing assumes a human operator per license; outcome pricing does not. → [Software](../03-domains/software.md)
- **Entry-level hiring in knowledge work visibly contracts.** This is the first period where the apprenticeship gap moves from prediction to measurable fact. → [Game 4](../02-games/4-labor.md)
- **Energy becomes the acknowledged bottleneck.** Electricity prices rise in datacenter-dense regions, generating real political backlash — and that backlash is a state-level force I have probably underweighted. → [Energy](../01-substrate/energy.md)
- **Capital markets test whether revenue is tracking capex.** The central question from [Compute](../01-substrate/compute.md) gets its first real answer.

> Probability of a significant AI-sector correction in this window: **~40%**

Note the likely *form* of a correction: consolidation and culling of secondary labs, not a halt to capability progress.

## What to watch

| Signal | Meaning |
|---|---|
| AI revenue run-rate vs. announced capex | Whether the correction lands |
| Entry-level : senior posting ratios | The labor transition beginning |
| Retail electricity prices in datacenter metros | Political backlash forming |
| Pricing model shifts (seat → outcome) | Agentic reliability crossing the commercial threshold |
